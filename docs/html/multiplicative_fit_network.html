Cluster scheduling simulator

The multiplicative fit network

The job encoder works as in the baseline and never sees usage. After each job has its 256-number summary, a fit attention step compares the job's usage with the machine's free room. Then every job looks at every other job, and the summaries are pooled and scored.

How the fit attention works

  • Inputs are two plain numbers. A job's usage is the largest cell in its usage matrix divided by 255 (0.25, 0.5 or 1.0). A machine's room is the smallest value of capacity minus usage over its whole window, divided by 255, which is how much space is left at its tightest moment.
  • Query, key, value. Small networks turn the job's usage into a query and the machine's room into a key and a value, each 64 numbers.
  • A sigmoid, not a softmax. With one machine a softmax is always 1, so every job would get the same answer. A sigmoid gives each job-machine pair its own weight between 0 and 1, which acts as "does this job fit here".
  • Output per job. The weight times the machine's value, summed over machines. It is 64 numbers that depend on both this job's usage and the machine's room.
  • Folded back in by multiplication. The 64 fit numbers are turned into 256 factors, one per channel of the job's summary. Each channel is multiplied by 1 + tanh(...), a number between 0 and 2, so a job's fit can turn parts of its summary down or up.

How the job self-attention works

  • Every job looks at every job. Each summary asks the other 10 summaries which ones are relevant to it and takes a weighted mix of them (4 heads, each looking for something different).
  • Why it comes after the fit step. By then each summary already includes how well that job fits the machine, so a job can compare itself with the other jobs on size, wait time and fit together, which is the comparison SJF makes.
  • Finished jobs are ignored as sources. Their old numbers would only add noise to the other jobs. Each job still keeps its own status through its own summary.
  • Same shape out. The result is added to the original summary and normalized, so the output is still 10 summaries of 256 numbers.
